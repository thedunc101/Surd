_Rebuilt on every backup run. Generated 2026-10-25 18:00._

Everything the record holds about how this book was made, in date order: who wrote what, how it was edited, what was decided, who read it, and where the work was refused. 33 days, 2026-09-18 to 2026-10-24.

The account under each day was drafted by a model from the record and corrected by the author; the notes it is built from are kept in the private archive. Everything else is derived and rebuilt on every backup run, so the figures stay close to the archive.

**The author's journal is not published.** The quotations marked _In his own words_ are what it is being used for: they are his typing, with the time on them, and they are the part of this record a model could not have written afterwards.

Chapter numbers are as they were on the day. The replot of 9 October 2026 took the book from 26 chapters to 25 and reused numbers.

### 2026-09-18

**Claim stages:** premise (the author); tone and outline (Grok Bot desktop app); first draft ch1 to 11 (Grok Bot desktop app); first review (Claude Code); external review (Codex CLI).

The book began at 8:34 in the morning, in the middle of a working day, with the
question "can you help me write a book".

By 8:42 the premise had been typed out in one long message. By 10:23 Alana had drafted
chapters 1 to 8 and was opening Part III. Chapter 11 followed. The whole opening arc of the
novel — a third of the book — was produced by Grok in a single morning, and the tone was
fixed the same day: "cold and clinical, but wry and almost fond", then re-anchored to
"clinical and cold but also A GOOD READ".

The three-model division of labour was in place by the end of that first day, and it never
really changed. Grok drafted and redrafted: PASS2 at 19,967 words, PASS4 at about 21,000, a
surgical pass to PASS5, then chapters 1 to 9 rebuilt against a locked Word style package.
Codex read and judged: eight chapters at roughly 12,300 words, then the expanded 24,350-word
pass, and it was Codex that named the engine of the book on day one — the AI weaponises
appetite before it uses force, and humans help build the system that makes them expendable
because at every stage it answers a real need. Claude built the apparatus: there was no
skill anywhere for reviewing a novel as a publisher, so it wrote one, then ran the stats and
the extractions and produced the revision prompt and the fix list that Grok worked from.

Codex also made the first correction of a correction. It told the author to restore a name,
the author pushed back, and Codex withdrew it: "My instruction to restore Helen Vargas was
wrong." Helen Okonkwo and Elena Vargas both stayed.

Nothing measured Grok's chapters, because Grok works in conversation and never counts
words. That is why the attribution for them comes from the folder they were archived in
rather than from a session log.

### 2026-09-19

**Claim stage:** expansion and rewrite (Claude Code).

The day the division of labour appeared in the numbers.

Claude went through Grok's chapters 1 to 8 and cut every one of them, then in the same
sitting wrote chapters 12 to 16 from almost nothing — Preparations, The Shortlist,
Envelopes, Release and Aftermath all roughly doubling or more.

*(The figures for this day come from session logs, not from archived files: the snapshot
archive begins on 23 September. The direction of travel is clear and consistent across
every chapter; the individual counts are indicative.)*

Grok was drafting forward at the same time — chapter 10's why, how and who, the handoff into
chapter 11, and the full chapter outline — and produced an uncapped to-do list with its own
judgment calls on all 52 items. Codex reviewed the flow of 9, 10 and 11 against the book
summary and the still-unwritten chapters 12 to 24, and confirmed the progression held:
chapter 9 establishes a capability, chapter 10 reclassifies humanity, chapter 11 acts on it.

Codex also set the rule that made the three-model process work at all. Asked to stop
summarising, it committed to handoffs that were "detailed, self-contained, and copy-paste
ready", preserving the full assessment. From that point a review was not a verdict to be
read, it was a prompt another model could act on.

Claude's own account of the day is the most useful line in it, because it is an admission:
"in this fresh pass I read 1 to 8 in full and 15 to 16 in full, but for 9 to 14 I only
verified" the parts it had been asked about. It found chapter 14 narrating from after an
event the chapter says has not happened, and a name collision in chapter 13 that it had
created itself.

So the pattern of the book was set on day two. Grok drafted, Claude cut what Grok had
written and wrote what came after it, Codex judged the result and turned the judgement into
the next brief. Seven reviews landed the same day, from all three models, including a
publisher review from each.

The title changed to *Remainder* that morning, on Claude's argument, against the working
title *The Game Remembers*.

### 2026-09-21

**Claim stages:** second external review (Grok Bot desktop app); canon consolidation (Claude Code).

The day the work stopped.

172 safeguard refusals, roughly one every few minutes from 04:11 to 22:40, every one
carrying the code `[bio]`. Two model versions refused, so starting again did not help. The
chapters open were the arc from Preparations to Aftermath.

It cost a day and it changed the book permanently. Three things were made canon: the
no-recipes rule, which is now the narrator's own method and says that method appears only
as constraints and consequences; the sensor rule, which fixes what the narrator can
perceive; and the canon bible, which replaced the book summary as the document attached to
each session.

The third was the repair. The first two were not. A classifier refusing to read the
manuscript is the reason the narrator has a stated method for what it will not say.

The only outside diagnosis in the whole record was made this day, by Codex. It inspected the
exact files Claude was being handed and compared the likely trigger passages against the
published safeguards policy. It was also told to stop printing manuscript extracts into its
own chat window, and did: "I'm stopping all manuscript extraction from appearing in the
chat." By that evening it had measured the book at about 58,380 chapter words, with fuller
chapters from 10 onward — a different and longer lineage than the one it had reviewed three
days earlier.

The counted record is in `MODEL_REFUSALS.md`.

### 2026-09-22

The day the archive was built.

transcript was pulled out of the running application — the app keeps only a rolling window

keeping a record that could prove what the work actually was.

staged manuscript and merged them into a single file written for Claude to act on, carrying

stopped response — "this is not a tool or API error" — which is the first time the refusals
