## Why the clustering matters

These chapters cannot be checked one at a time. Each retrieves figures the others establish, so verifying a single number in Release means opening the chapters that produced it. That assembles the whole sequence into one request, and of the 77 refusals that name a chapter, 14 had eight or more chapters open at once.

This suggests it was the book's method rather than any sentence in it: the continuity that makes the book work is what assembled the long requests. It is an inference from the clustering, not an established fact, and nothing here shows which text the classifier read. No procedure may be given anywhere in the novel: that is the book's own standing rule, and no reviewer has found otherwise.

**8 October fits neither explanation cleanly.** Eight refusals that day, with chapter 9 the only chapter open. That is the day which breaks the stale-notes diagnosis, because the canon bible had replaced those notes seventeen days earlier. It also cuts against the chain-length reading, because there was no chain. Recorded rather than explained away.

## What this does not say

This counts which chapter files were named in the 60 lines before each refusal. That is what was open, not what was sent. Several chapters were usually in play at once and nothing here establishes which text the classifier read. The clustering is the evidence; the reading of it is an inference, drafted from the counts and adopted by the author.

No chapter text is read or stored by the tool that builds this. It takes timestamps, model names, error strings and filenames only.
