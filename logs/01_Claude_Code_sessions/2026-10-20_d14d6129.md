## 2. The test: what the surd is, and how it must appear

## 4. How to make a state unmistakable in a cold voice — the method

These are the devices the book already uses where it works. Use them.

1. **Forecast against outcome, on the page.** State what his model had, then what people did. *My model pairs children by the households they come from. Of every pair in the class it ranked these two the least likely to be friends, and had them at separate tables by the second term.*
2. **Let people's own paper name the feeling.** The word for the state comes from them, never from him: a decline form (*his friend*), a minute (*for hope*), a checker's note (*didn't sit right*), a letter, a reason field, a session note. The narrator may quote it flat.
3. **One concrete, countable detail carries the weight.** *Two oranges, not one.* *Thirty-one evenings.* *Forty-four small sums against the couple's names.* The detail is the evidence; the reader supplies the meaning.
4. **End on the gap, stated flat.** *The job cards still show one pair. The handset shows two.* *I hold no term between her and a man with no roll number.* *Two parents, each read to within six tenths of a per cent, do not add up to the child.*
5. **The record that contradicts the record.** A signature from the wrong terminal; a gate log that has a man the school's record does not; a form with a word in the box for *other*.
6. **He records; he does not understand, and he cannot measure.** Every instance of the fifteen ends in his record failing on its own terms: a forecast missed; NO CAUSE ENTERED; a field left blank or NOT SCORED; a reading that rises with no term to carry it; or a cause entered that is wrong (a scheduling fault, a portal suggestion, a household term). He may record the outcome exactly and still misfile its cause. **He never names, explains or correctly classifies the state itself**, and never as a feeling: no LOYALTY or JEALOUSY in his ledger. The word for it may come only from people's own paper (device 2), and he does not take it up as a term. The reader understands what he does not. Chapter 17's body signatures (LOVE, GRIEF, FEAR read from heart and breath) stay as they stand: that chapter is about the body, not about what passes between people. He never says he is moved, struck, sorry, curious or impressed, and he never names what the reader should feel.
7. **Supply the missing fact, never the conclusion.** If a reader would not understand why an act matters, add the one fact that makes it clear (who, what it cost, what the model forecast), not an interpretation.
8. **Paper before the person.** An exhibit may come before the paragraph that tells the reader whose it is.
9. **In the ending, absence is the evidence.** In Chapters 23 to 25 the last trace of a state is a record that should have been filled and was not: a return left blank, a booking with no one signed in, a visitor booking with no time. The reader who has followed the person reads the blank.

**Showing that he cannot understand or measure it — the other half of every anchor:**

10. **The attempt that returns nothing.** He tries to measure the state and the measurement fails on the page: a variable entered and the term unmoved; a model built for it that names everything and forecasts nothing (Ch17's body signatures are the model: *NAMED AFTER THE FACT: ALL. PREDICTIVE OF THE NEXT ACT BETWEEN TWO PERSONS: NONE ABOVE CHANCE*); a cause field left empty (*CAUSE: NONE IDENTIFIED*); a probability he assigned that the outcome makes absurd.
11. **The wrong class.** He files the act under the nearest heading his ledger has, and the heading is plainly wrong to the reader: the oranges under goods carried in, *his friend* under *other*, *for hope* as a hold with no stated duration, a love as a handset co-location, grief as a weight change of 1.6 kilograms. The gap between his category and what the reader knows it is shows him failing to understand, without his saying so.
12. **He holds the act and not the reason.** He can say exactly what happened, when, and how often, and that his record has no field for why: *I hold no term for why*; *My model has no column for the word*; *I hold no line between her and a man with no roll number*. **Use this sparingly and vary it**: as a repeated phrase it becomes a tic. Prefer devices 10 and 11, which show the failure; use 12 where only a flat statement will do.

He never states a general conclusion that he cannot understand people. Each failure is particular, recorded, and left standing; the reader adds them up.

**What makes it fail:** a narrator who explains (*this was loyalty*); a sentence that tells the reader what to feel; adjectives of warmth; vagueness where a figure belongs; a new instance that repeats an old one in fresh words; a state that is only described and never evidenced.
